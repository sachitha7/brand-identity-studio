# Client intake

Discovery is the stage most often skipped, and skipping it is why brand work
comes back as "not what I wanted." The client holds information you cannot
research — why they started, which customers they'd fire, what the last
agency got wrong. Ask for it in writing, early.

Send the questionnaire at Stage 1, before Stage 2 begins. Give the client a
week. Chase once.

## What to generate for the client

Produce these as PDFs into `00-brief/`:

1. **Brand questionnaire** — the form below, for the client to complete
2. **Stakeholder interview guide** — for live sessions with founders/staff
3. **Asset request list** — what you need from them to start

## The brand questionnaire

Adapt the wording to the client's sector, keep the substance.

**The business**
1. In one sentence a customer would use — what do you sell, and to whom?
2. When did you start, and why? What was the gap?
3. What do you actually make yourself versus buy in or outsource?
4. What's your price position — cheaper, comparable, or premium against who?
5. What's the single biggest constraint on growth right now?

**Customers**
6. Describe your best customer. Not demographics — what's their situation
   when they buy?
7. Who buys from you that you'd rather not serve, and why?
8. Where do customers currently find you?
9. What do customers say when they're pleased? Quote them if you can.
10. What's the most common objection or hesitation before buying?

**Competition**
11. Name three competitors. For each: what do they do better than you?
12. What can you credibly claim that none of them can?
13. Which brand in *any* sector do you admire, and what specifically about it?

**The brand today**
14. What exists already — logo, colours, packaging, website, social?
15. What's working about it, and what embarrasses you?
16. Has anything been tried before that failed? What happened?
17. Who signs off on brand decisions, and who else has opinions that matter?

**Where it's going**
18. Where do you want the business to be in three years?
19. Are you planning to export, franchise, or add product lines?
20. What would make this project a success in your words?

**Practicalities**
21. Deadline, and what's driving it?
22. Budget range for production — printing, packaging tooling, signage?
23. Any legal constraints — existing trademarks, regulated claims, required
    label fields?

## Stakeholder interview guide

Run 30–45 minutes each with the founder, someone customer-facing, and, if
possible, a long-standing customer. Interview separately; group sessions
produce consensus, not truth.

Open with: *"Tell me how the business started."* Let it run.

Then probe:
- What do you say when someone asks what you do?
- What do you wish customers understood that they don't?
- Tell me about a customer who left. What happened?
- If the business disappeared tomorrow, what would customers miss?
- What are we most likely to get wrong in this project?

That last question is worth the whole interview. Ask it of everyone.

## Asset request list

Ask for, and confirm you have the rights to use:

- Logo in the highest quality available — vector (AI/EPS/SVG) if it exists,
  otherwise the largest PNG or the original design file
- Any existing brand guidelines, even partial or outdated
- Product photography, and whether it was shot or supplied by a designer
- Product list with names, sizes, prices, key claims
- Existing packaging artwork as print files if available
- Website and social handles
- Registered company name, address, contact details exactly as they should
  appear in print
- Certifications or regulatory marks that must appear on packaging

**Check what you're given.** Client-supplied "artwork" is frequently a
concept render rather than production files — rendered text in it can be
garbled or placeholder. Read every word of any supplied artwork before
reproducing it, and flag what's wrong rather than carrying the error
forward into a deliverable.

## When the client doesn't answer

Common, especially with small businesses. Proceed on what you have, and
make the gaps explicit in the deliverable rather than inventing answers:
state the assumption on the page, flag it as needing confirmation before
print or launch. A brand book that says "these active percentages are
targets pending formulator confirmation" is more useful than one that
invents numbers and looks finished.
